1. Why Switch to a Waveform: The Dilemma of OFDM in "Time Delay + Doppler" Channel Conditions
Let's first clarify the problem itself. Wireless channels exhibit dual selectivity under high-speed movement: on one hand, multipath causes time delay expansion, and on the other hand, relative motion between the target/transmitter and receiver causes Doppler expansion. After passing through such a "dual-selective channel," signals can be modeled as superposition of several paths:
The sampling time delay for each path, normalized Doppler frequency (i.e., Doppler frequency divided by the sampling rate), and the complex path gain, along with additive Gaussian white noise.
OFDM relies on cyclic prefix and subcarrier orthogonality to combat multipath, but its orthogonality is predicated on the premise that "the subcarrier spacing is much larger than the Doppler spread." As Doppler increases, inter-subcarrier interference (ICI) arises, leading to a rapid degradation in OFDM's bit error rate performance—a phenomenon clearly evident in subsequent practical comparisons. OTFS, while shifting symbols to the time-delay-Doppler domain, has a natural advantage under high Doppler conditions. However, it requires Heisenberg/Weigner transforms and two-dimensional grid mapping, which introduces significant engineering complexity and sensitivity to the grid resolution for time delay and Doppler.
The approach of AFDM is to modulate in the "chirp domain": it uses **discrete affine Fourier transform (DAFT)** as the core transformation, introducing two chirp phase parameters that inherently make the waveform robust to parameter perturbations across the entire chirp domain. This direction also holds considerable potential for candidate waveforms in the 6G spectrum.
